One year ago yesterday, I hit “publish” on the very first edition of The CyberSafe Brief.

My mission was simple: I wanted to sharpen my ability to translate “tech jargon” into plain English and help my friends and family navigate a complex digital world.

I’m thrilled to say this project has grown beyond my wildest expectations. It helped me earn a recent promotion, and my team at my day job has even decided to adapt a similar newsletter format to distribute to all 25,000 employees at our organization.

But whether I’m writing for 25 people or 25,000, the goal remains the same: small, actionable advice for everyday life.

___

## The Year in Review: How Attacks Changed

Looking back at our archive, 2025 was a year of rapid shifts. Here are the three biggest trends we tackled:

- **The Rise of AI Deception**: We moved from spotting bad grammar to avoiding sophisticated “AI Browsers” and malicious extensions that can quietly read your private chats.
- **The Year of “ClickFix”**: We saw a massive rise in social engineering attacks - like the Fake Blue Screens we discussed in January - where pop-ups trick you into copy-pasting code to “fix” an issue, unknowingly infecting your own computer. 
- **The End of Old Favorites**: We watched the countdown on Windows 10 support and learned that even old, trusted tools like WinRAR can become dangerous if ignored.

## Secure-in-Sixty Challenge

To celebrate, I’m challenging you to take 60 seconds right now to complete three spring cleaning tasks:

1. Check for “The Pwn”: Visit HaveIBeenPwned.com and enter your email. If it’s red, change that password (and consider a password manager!).
2. The “Ghost” App Hunt: Delete one app on your phone you haven’t opened in three months. Fewer apps mean fewer possible data leaks.
3. Update One Thing: Whether it’s Chrome, iOS, or your laptop, run one manual update check right now.

To everyone who has read, replied, and shared this newsletter over the last 12 months: **Thank you**. I look forward to another year of helping each other stay secure.
