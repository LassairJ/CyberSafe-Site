## Windows 10 Support Ends. Is Your PC Ready?
Microsoft will officially end support for Windows 10 on **October 14, 2025**. That means no more free security patches, feature updates, or technical help for most users. After that date, your PC may still work, but any new software vulnerabilities discovered won’t be fixed, making your machine a target for hackers.

Microsoft is offering Extended Security Updates (ESU) as a paid option to keep getting security fixes beyond the deadline; In Europe, this ESU will be free for consumers until October 2026 under certain conditions. But elsewhere, you may need to pay, or link your Windows account to Microsoft for eligibility.

___

### What to Know
- October 14 is the cutoff: after that, Windows 10 devices will no longer get official support.
- Unsupported systems are more vulnerable to attacks.
- ESU gives you a safety net, but it’s temporary and won’t include new features.
- Some users may not be able to upgrade to Windows 11 due to hardware limits.

### What to Do Now
- Check if your PC can run **Windows 11** (go to *Settings → Update & Security*).
- If it’s eligible, **upgrade before the deadline**.
- If it’s not eligible, enroll in **ESU** to keep security updates.
- Back up all your important files in case anything goes wrong.
- Plan for the day your PC must be upgraded; older hardware might need replacing.
   

[Microsoft - "Windows 10 support ends on October 14, 2025"](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/deployment/updates-lifecycle/windows-10-support-has-ended-on-october-14-2025)

## Discord Exposes 70,000 Users’ Government IDs
Discord revealed that about 70,000 users may have had their **government ID photos** exposed in a data breach tied to a third-party service used for age verification. This didn’t happen through Discord’s main system; rather, the breach occurred through a vendor they used for processing ID verification requests when users couldn’t access accounts. Exposed data may also include names, email addresses, IP addresses, parts of support messages, and other contact details.

### What to Know
- 70,000 users’ government-issued ID photos may have been leaked.
- The breach was due to a third-party vendor, not a direct hack of Discord’s core systems.
- Other personal data tied to those users - emails, IP addresses, support messages - may also have been exposed.

### What to Do Now
- If you’ve submitted an ID to Discord for verification, check for alerts from them.
- Be cautious of phishing attempts using your personal info.
- Monitor accounts linked to your email for suspicious activity.
- Limit sharing of sensitive documents in online services unless you trust them and know how your data will be handled.
