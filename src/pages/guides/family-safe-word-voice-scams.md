## What's happening

It now takes only a few seconds of someone's voice — from a voicemail, a
social media video, even a "can you hear me?" robocall — for AI to produce a
convincing clone of it.

Scammers are using this to supercharge the old "grandparent scam." The call
sounds exactly like your child or grandchild: _"Mom, I've been in an accident,
I need you to wire money right now, please don't tell Dad."_ It's urgent,
emotional, and designed to make you act before you think.

## Why a safe word works

A scammer can fake a voice, but they can't guess a secret word your family
agreed on in person. It's low-tech, free, and it works for everyone —
including grandparents who will never install a security app.

## Do this now

- [ ] **Pick a word or short phrase** that's easy to remember but hard to
      guess. Avoid pet names, birthdays, and anything posted on social media.
      Two words beat one: "blueberry pancake" is better than "apple."
- [ ] **Share it in person or on a live video call** — not by text or email,
      which can be hacked, forwarded, or faked.
- [ ] **Make sure everyone who might get "the call" knows it**: parents,
      grandparents, partners, and kids old enough to have a phone.
- [ ] **Agree on the rule**: any urgent request for money or personal
      information by phone gets the safe-word question first. No exceptions,
      no embarrassment about asking.
- [ ] **Practice once.** Call a family member this week and run through it,
      so it feels normal before it matters.

## When you get a scary call

1. **Don't panic, don't send money, don't share any codes.**
2. **Ask for the safe word.** A real family member will know it instantly.
3. **If they can't give it, hang up** — then call the person back on their
   real number, the one already in your contacts.
4. Remember: even if the voice sounds exactly right, voices can be faked.
   Safe words can't be guessed.
